# Health app for the Life hub: UI/UX, Samsung Health / Health Connect export, and self-hosted pipelines (as of 2026-11-06)

Scope: one self-hosted "Health" app inside Odysseus/Life, single user in Brazil, Samsung Android phone (maybe a Galaxy Watch), NAS with NixOS/Docker and Home Assistant, Google Fit Takeout history (2018–2026) already in hand. Research done 2026-11-06. Third-party vendor blogs (fitmesh.fit, sahha.ai, takeoutday.org, etc.) are flagged where used. Some facts come only from search-result snippets because the pages returned 403. Those are marked "(snippet)".

## 1. UI/UX: what a personal Health app should show, and how the reference apps do it

### Takeaway
All the major apps (Apple Health, Samsung Health, Garmin Connect, Google Fit, Oura and WHOOP) have settled on the same layout. The top is a short, user-chosen set of "today" tiles: pinned metrics plus one composite readiness or energy score. Below that sit generated highlights and trends, comparing this week or month with a baseline. Under everything is a full "browse by category" catalogue with day/week/month/year charts per metric. The most common complaints are a home screen the user can't control (Samsung 2026) and lots of data with little interpretation (Apple, older critiques).

### Cited Findings

**Apple Health (current iOS 18/26 structure)**
- The Summary tab has a user-curated section of prioritized metrics. In iOS 18 it was renamed from "Favorites" to "Pinned", and you edit it via "Edit" next to Pinned — [igeeksblog guide](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-health-app-on-iphone/); [Apple support: Use the Health app](https://support.apple.com/104997)
- Below Pinned, a "Highlights" section shows graphs comparing recent health data, with a "Show All Highlights" link. Further down are "Trends" with a "View Health Trends" link — [Apple iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-your-health-data-iphe3d379c32/15.0); [Apple support 104997](https://support.apple.com/104997)
- Older critique (2019, MacStories): favorites could not be reordered. I found no source confirming whether current iOS allows reordering — [MacStories iOS 13 Health](https://www.macstories.net/?p=60534)
- A long-running criticism is that the Health app is "a repository of data" that rarely interprets trends. Macworld contrasted it unfavourably with the Apple Watch Activity rings app. These are older opinion pieces — [Macworld: Apple's Health app really needs a redesign](https://www.macworld.com/article/225137/opinion-apples-health-app-really-needs-a-redesign.html); [IDEO: We're drowning in health data](https://www.ideo.com/journal/were-drowning-in-health-data)

- **Readiness score**: one 0–10 score per day with four recommendations (Recover / Pace Yourself / Ready / Go For It). Inputs are recent activity, vitals and sleep score. It updates through the day and shows which factors are driving it — [Apple Newsroom](https://apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-advances-health-and-fitness-capabilities-using-apple-intelligence/)
- Availability: it launches "starting in U.S. English, with more languages to follow". It was in the iOS 27.3 developer beta as of early November 2026 and is not yet shipped to the public. **Samsung Health (7.0 redesign, June 2026)**

The home dashboard groups Sleep, Activity, Nutrition, Mindfulness and Vitals, with daily wellness tips and an AI "Energy Score" (snippet) — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/samsung-health-app-redesign-arrives-june-8-with-home-dashboard/tldr); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-samsung-health-watch-features/); [Samsung US Newsroom](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-health-app-evolves-with-ai-powered-proactive-intelligence-to-help-users-stay-ahead-of-their-health/)

**Garmin Connect (v5, 2024 redesign)**
- The home screen is split into sections: Today's Activity, In Focus, and At a Glance (up to 8 health widgets). Users can rearrange or hide sections via "Edit Home" — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Garmin-Connect-app-home-screen-refresh-now-available-for-all-users.830682.0.html); [Wareable hands-on](https://www.wareable.com/garmin/garmin-connect-app-update-2024-hands-on)
- Criticism: larger cards fit less on screen, goal colour coding was toned down, and users have to scroll more. Android Authority praised it as "simplification without sacrifice" — [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/?p=15579975); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/garmin-connect-redesign-3364392)

**Readiness / recovery scores (Oura, WHOOP, Garmin)**
- Oura Readiness is a 0–100 morning score with listed contributors (resting HR, HRV balance, temperature deviation, sleep). WHOOP Recovery is 0–100% and HRV-heavy. Garmin has a 0–100 morning Training Readiness score (which includes training load) and a continuous Body Battery gauge that drains and recharges through the day. Sources are vendor or affiliate blogs — [sensai.fit](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/garmin-body-battery-vs-whoop-recovery-vs-oura-readiness-how-calculated-2026); [athletedata.health](https://athletedata.health/guides/whoop-vs-oura-vs-garmin); [kygo.app](https://kygo.app/post/recovery-scores-compared-whoop-oura-garmin)
- In one review of 14 composite scores from 10 makers, 86% used HRV. No vendor publishes its formula, and there is no head-to-head validation (secondary source) — [gneta.app](https://www.gneta.app/blog/recovery-scores-compared-garmin-whoop-oura)
- Advice: don't average scores across devices, because they weight different inputs and sample at different times (vendor blog) — [vitra-health](https://vitra-health.vercel.app/blog/oura-vs-whoop-vs-garmin-recovery)
- Some users find Body Battery and stress unhelpful. Others find Body Battery more actionable than WHOOP's score — [TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/whoop-vs-oura-for-recovery-and-sleep-trends/113593)

**Canonical data types (what the platforms model)**
- Health Connect's categories are Activity, Body Measurement, Cycle Tracking, Nutrition, Sleep, Vitals, and Wellness (mindfulness). Each record is either Interval (steps, distance, sleep, exercise, nutrition, hydration), Instantaneous (weight, BP, SpO2, HRV-RMSSD, resting HR, VO2max, body temperature, glucose), or Series (heart rate, speed, power, cadence, skin temperature) — [Android Developers: Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)
- Health Connect provides aggregate functions per type, such as `COUNT_TOTAL` for steps, `BPM_AVG/MIN/MAX` for heart rate, `SYSTOLIC_AVG` for blood pressure, `WEIGHT_AVG` and `SLEEP_DURATION_TOTAL`, and more than 40 nutrient totals — [Android Developers: data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)

### Inferences
- **Recommended Health app layout for Odysseus (desktop-first):**
  1. **"Hoje" strip**: 4–8 pinned tiles chosen by the user (steps vs goal, active minutes or "Heart Points-style" intensity minutes, sleep last night, resting HR, latest weight, latest BP). Each tile shows its value, a 7-day sparkline and a delta against a 28-day baseline. The pinned list must be user-editable and reorderable: Apple's old no-reorder limit and Samsung 7.0's non-removable cards are the most cited pain points.
  2. **Highlights**: auto-generated, factual comparisons ("Você andou 18% mais este mês que a média de 6 meses", "Sono médio caiu 25 min esta semana"). Use templated rules rather than an AI coach, which is cheaper, verifiable and fits privacy-first. Make highlights dismissible.
  3. **Browse/catalogue by category** (Activity, Sleep, Heart/Vitals, Body, Workouts, Nutrition, Cycle) mirroring Health Connect categories. Each metric page gets a D/W/M/6M/Y/All range switch with the right chart per record type. Interval totals (steps, distance) use bars. Instantaneous values (weight, BP, RHR) use dots plus a rolling mean line. BP shows systolic/diastolic range bars. Sleep uses a hypnogram per night and stacked stage bars over time. Series HR shows a min–max band.
  4. **Long-range view**: the 2018–2026 Google Fit history is the app's unique asset. Year-over-year views such as a calendar heatmap of steps or move minutes by year are something phone apps do poorly and a desktop app can do well.
- A composite "readiness" score is **not** recommended for v1. Formulas are proprietary and unvalidated, and Samsung's HRV doesn't reliably reach Health Connect (see section 2). Showing the raw contributors (sleep duration, RHR vs baseline, HRV if present) is more honest.
- Priority of metrics for this user, given the data actually available: steps/distance/move minutes (2018–2026 Takeout) > workouts/sessions > sleep (if Galaxy Watch) > resting HR / HR > weight/body composition > BP/SpO2 > nutrition/cycle (only if logged).

### Gaps
- No current (2025–2026) usability research on Apple's Summary vs Browse. Critiques found are mostly 2016–2019 opinion pieces.
- Android Authority's 2026 Samsung Health hands-on couldn't be fetched (403). Its details come from search snippets only.
- Couldn't confirm whether the new Samsung Health 7.x home lets users reorder or hide the top cards as of November 2026 (complaints say no, as of September 2026).

## 2. Samsung Health export: "Download personal data", Health Connect sync, Galaxy Watch specifics

### Takeaway
Samsung Health has an official in-app "Download personal data" option (Settings → Download personal data). It writes a folder of per-metric CSVs plus `jsons/` (per-minute binned data) and `files/` (for example ECG waveforms) to `My Files/Downloads/Samsung Health/` on the phone. This is the only path for full history and Samsung-proprietary metrics. The Samsung Health ↔ Health Connect sync covers a documented core set (steps, exercise, HR, sleep, weight/body fat/BMR/height, BP, glucose, SpO2, nutrition). It does **not** reliably carry HRV, stress, ECG, skin temperature, or Samsung's own scores.

### Cited Findings

**Official export procedure (Samsung)**
- Samsung Health → ⋮ More options → Settings → "Download personal data" → Download → grant permissions and sign in with Samsung account → wait → OK. Files go to **My Files → Downloads → Samsung Health → [data folder]**. Samsung says you can download your data "at any time". The page doesn't state the format. Page dated "Last Update: Jul 24, 2026" — [Samsung Canada support](https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/apps-services/download-or-delete-your-samsung-health-data/)
- Erasing data is a separate option in the same menu, and it deletes data on the phone, in the app, and on Samsung servers — [Samsung support (search snippet)](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10001379)
- **Conflict:** a third-party guide (updated September 2026) describes a different flow. It says the menu is Privacy settings / Data management → "Request download", you choose CSV or JSON, and the ZIP link is emailed within about 24h and expires in a few days. Its sample file names (`com.samsung.health.<category>.json`) don't match the official on-device flow or the parser libraries below. Treat it as unreliable — [takeoutday.org guide](https://takeoutday.org/guides/how-to-export-samsung-health-data)

**Export structure (from parser libraries and community)**
- The export is a directory with "CSV files per health metric (some with a metadata row, some without)", a **`jsons/`** subfolder of per-minute binning JSON, and a **`files/`** subfolder of binary attachments (ECG waveforms, photos) — [samsung-health-sdk 0.2.6 on PyPI, Apr 2026](https://pypi.org/project/samsung-health-sdk/)
- Parsing quirks the library handles: UTF-8 **BOM**, **namespaced column headers** (for example `com.samsung.health.heart_rate.heart_rate`), **UTC-offset timestamps**, and **trailing commas**. Sleep stage codes are 40001 Awake, 40002 Light, 40003 Deep, 40004 REM. Metrics exposed include HR (minute or hourly), sleep, skin temperature, stress, SpO2, steps, HRV, respiratory rate, exercise and per-minute movement — [samsung-health-sdk PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/samsung-health-sdk/)
- A sample sleep CSV has a `time_offset` column with values like `UTC+0300` and the package name `com.sec.android.app.shealth` (2018 example) — [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/dimshik100/0ea44dbe3895553ecdac219ddf64f146)
- Exercise data is split across several CSVs sharing the `com.samsung.shealth.exercise` prefix. Some timestamps are epoch milliseconds, and a user saw a date/time column open as a large integer in Excel. The thread notes the structure has changed over time — [Samsung Developer forum: export field descriptions](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/samsung-health-export-files-field-descriptions/1727); [Samsung Community](https://r1.community.samsung.com/t5/galaxy-s/samsung-health-data-export/m-p/17305236/highlight/true)
- A community "data dictionary" request thread exists. Samsung does not publish an official schema for the export — [Samsung Developer forum: Data Dictionary](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/data-dictionary-for-files-exported-from-samsung-health/17283)
- Other parsers: `davidmosiah/samsung-health-mcp` is a local MCP server reading the CSV or ZIP export, inferring record types from filenames and headers. `ReadableCode/Health_Data_Parser` is another — [samsung-health-mcp](https://github.com/davidmosiah/samsung-health-mcp); [Health_Data_Parser](https://github.com/ReadableCode/Health_Data_Parser)
- Historical note: in 2019 DC Rainmaker said S Health had no CSV export and pointed to GPX per workout. That is outdated — [DC Rainmaker 2019](https://dcrainmaker.com/2019/03/export-samsung-galaxy.html)

**Samsung Health ↔ Health Connect sync (official mapping)**
- Samsung's developer blog (Feb 18, 2025) lists the types synced in both directions: Steps → `StepsRecord`; Blood glucose → `BloodGlucoseRecord`; Blood oxygen → `OxygenSaturationRecord`; Blood pressure → `BloodPressureRecord`; Exercise session/calories/distance/HR/power/speed/VO2max → `ExerciseSessionRecord`/`TotalCaloriesBurnedRecord`/`DistanceRecord`/`HeartRateRecord`/`PowerRecord`/`SpeedRecord`/`Vo2MaxRecord`; Heart rate → `HeartRateRecord`; Nutrition → `NutritionRecord`; Sleep session and stages → `SleepSessionRecord`; Weight, body fat, BMR, height → `WeightRecord`/`BodyFatRecord`/`BasalMetabolicRateRecord`/`HeightRecord`. The blog notes scope "can be changed depending on the Samsung Health version" and that "activity tracker data is not synchronized" — [Samsung Developer blog: Accessing Samsung Health data through Health Connect](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)
- HC sync has been supported since Samsung Health 6.22.5 (October 2022). It runs phone-side and requires "Sync with Samsung Account" to be enabled — [Samsung Developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect); [CareClinic KB](https://start.careclinic.io/knowledgebase/integrations/sync-my-samsung-health-data/374/)
- **Not shared to Health Connect:** ECG readings, training-recovery data and HRV (snippet; page 403) — [Android Authority: Google Health Connect](https://androidauthority.com/google-health-connect-3164755). A user reports HRV, breathing rate and resting HR not reaching HC while other metrics sync — [Samsung Community thread](https://us.community.samsung.com/t5/Galaxy-Watch/HRV-breathing-rate-and-resting-HR-won-t-write-to-the-health/td-p/3351258)
- Samsung-proprietary metrics such as body composition (BIA) and the continuous stress score don't pass through HC, and historical data isn't backfilled. A manual export is always needed (vendor blog and APK notes) — [fitmesh.fit (vendor)](https://www.fitmesh.fit/en/blog/sync-samsung-health-google-fit); [Health Sync release notes via APKMirror](https://www.apkmirror.com/?p=13623995)
- Since 2020, Samsung has not let partner apps write **steps** into Samsung Health. Reading steps and writing other types still works (per Health Sync's notes) — [APKMirror Health Sync notes](https://www.apkmirror.com/?p=13623995)
- Reliability: developers report unreliable or delayed SH → HC sync, for example HR from a Galaxy Watch4 not syncing — [Samsung Dev forum: sync unreliable](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/syncing-data-is-unreliable-between-samsung-health-and-health-connect/24850); [Samsung Dev forum: HR sync](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/heart-rate-sync-with-google-health-connect/24632); [Samsung Dev forum: Health Connect delay](https://forum.developer.samsung.com/t/health-connect-delay/35955)
- The legacy Samsung Health SDK for Android is deprecated. Samsung points developers to Health Connect or the newer Samsung Health Data SDK — [Samsung Health developer overview](https://developer.samsung.com/health/overview.html); [Samsung Health Data SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/data/overview.html)

**Galaxy Watch specifics**
- Watch data reaches the phone's Samsung Health only when the watch reconnects, when the phone app home is opened, or on pull-to-refresh. Continuous HR is deliberately not synced immediately to save battery, while manual HR measurements are — [Samsung Developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)
- The watch writes to HC only through the phone's Samsung Health. If SH lacks HC write permission, nothing arrives. Battery optimisation on SH and Galaxy Wearable is a common culprit (vendor blog) — [fitmesh.fit (vendor)](https://www.fitmesh.fit/en/blog/health-connect-not-syncing)
- **Brazil:** Anvisa approved Samsung's BP and ECG apps (Samsung Health Monitor) as Software as a Medical Device in August 2020, registrations 81549259004 (BP) and 81549259006 (ECG). The BP app must be calibrated against an Anvisa-approved cuff and cannot diagnose hypertension — [Samsung Newsroom Brasil](https://news.samsung.com/br/novo-aplicativo-samsung-health-monitor-realiza-medicao-de-pressao-e-eletrocardiograma/1000); [Tecnoblog](https://tecnoblog.net/360849/apps-da-samsung-para-pressao-e-ecg-sao-aprovados-pela-anvisa/); [ICTQ](https://ictq.com.br/assuntos-regulatorios/anvisa-aprova-aplicativos-para-eletrocardiograma-e-pressao-arterial)
- Irregular heart rhythm notification for Galaxy Watch6 was approved by Anvisa in September 2023 — [Samsung Newsroom Brasil](https://news.samsung.com/br/notificacao-de-frequencia-cardiaca-irregular-do-samsung-galaxy-watch6-e-aprovada-pela-anvisa-no-brasil); [Tecnoblog 2023](https://tecnoblog.net/noticias/2023/09/26/samsung-libera-no-brasil-recurso-do-galaxy-watch-6-para-detectar-problema-cardiaco/)
- 2026 redesign complaints include watch-sync failures (193 of 773 redesign complaints) and "couldn't open Samsung health on phone" — [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/samsung-health-update-not-syncing-watch-layout-reviews-2026)

### Inferences
- **Importer design for Odysseus (Samsung):** accept the export folder or a ZIP of it. Detect each CSV's metric from the filename prefix (`com.samsung.shealth.*` / `com.samsung.health.*`). Skip an optional first metadata line, strip the BOM and the namespace prefix from headers, tolerate trailing commas, and combine UTC `start_time` with the `time_offset` column (`UTC-0300`) to produce local wall-clock time. Parse `jsons/` lazily, only for the minute-level HR/steps detail. Treat `files/` (ECG) as attachments. Upsert idempotently, keyed by Samsung's `datauuid` column if present (unverified, check a real export).
- **Expected overlap with Google Fit Takeout:** if Samsung Health synced steps to Fit (via HC or Health Sync), daily step totals in Takeout and in the Samsung export will double-count. The app needs a per-day "source of truth" priority, for example Samsung Health > Google Fit for days where both exist.
- Re-export cadence: there's no documented limit, so a monthly or quarterly manual export is a reasonable fallback for Samsung-only metrics (HRV, stress, body composition, ECG) that never reach Health Connect.

### Gaps
- No official Samsung schema or data dictionary for the CSV export. The exact file naming (`com.samsung.shealth.<metric>.<yyyymmddhhmmss>.csv`) and the presence of `datauuid` are from memory and community usage, not verified by a 2026 source. Verify against a real export.
- Whether the 2026 Samsung Health 7.x still writes the export to `Downloads/Samsung Health/` vs an emailed ZIP is uncertain. The official Samsung CA page (July 2026) says on-device. One third-party guide says emailed ZIP.
- Whether menstrual cycle, stress or Energy Score are included in the 2026 export could not be confirmed.
- The exact current list of SH → HC types under Samsung Health 7.x (2026) is unpublished. The February 2025 blog is the latest official mapping.

## 3. Health Connect as the hub: automatic export to a server

### Takeaway
Health Connect (HC) is an on-device store: Android 14+ system module, Play app on Android 9–13. It has **no official server API**. Getting data out continuously needs an Android app holding HC read permissions plus the special `READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` and `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` permissions (the default is the last 30 days). Open-source options that work today include **Health Connect Webhook** (AGPL, 31 types, JSON/Protobuf to webhooks, plus a LAN HTTP server), the **Home Assistant Companion app's 36 Health Connect sensors**, and community HA bridges. Health Sync is the commercial alternative. HC's built-in backup is cloud ZIP only.

### Cited Findings

**Platform facts**
- Reading in the background and reading data older than 30 days require separate permissions: `android.permission.health.READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` and `android.permission.health.READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` — [Android Developers: HC data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)
- By default HC exposes only the preceding 30 days of other apps' data. Older records need the history permission, which the user can refuse — [Sahha (vendor) summarising Google docs](https://sahha.ai/blog/google-fit-api-sunset-migration/); [Android Developers: read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/read-data)
- Samsung's blog states that HC data can only be retrieved by foreground apps "for security reasons". This predates the background-read permission and appears outdated — [Samsung Developer blog, Feb 2025](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)
- Health Connect is a system app since Android 14 (client API ≥ 1.1.0). It works on Android 9+ via the Play app — [Samsung Developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)
- **Built-in backup:** Settings → Security & privacy → Health Connect → Manage data → Backup and restore. It schedules daily, weekly or monthly export of a ZIP ("Health Connect.zip") to Google Drive or another cloud provider. It requires Android 14+, and medical records are not included. It rolled out November 2024 — [Google support: Back up and restore your Health Connect data](https://support.google.com/android/answer/15323271?hl=EN-GB); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/health-connect-data-backup-rolling-out-3498985/)
- You "can't export the database locally", only schedule cloud exports. Early users reported export problems (snippet) — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/health-connect-data-backup-3498985/); [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2024/11/google-health-connect-gets-backup-function-for-health-data.html)
- HC also stores **medical records** (FHIR-based) alongside fitness data — [Android Developers: HC data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)

**Open-source exporters**
- **Health Connect Webhook** (`mcnaveen/health-connect-webhook`): 31 types, including steps, sleep with stages, HR, HRV-RMSSD, distance, active and total calories, weight, height, BP, glucose, SpO2, body and skin temperature, respiratory rate, RHR, exercise sessions, hydration, nutrition, BMR, body fat, lean mass, VO2max, bone mass, menstruation/ovulation/cervical mucus/sexual activity, basal body temperature and mindfulness. It sends JSON or Protobuf/gRPC per webhook. Background sync runs at an interval (WorkManager) or fixed times (AlarmManager), plus a widget, a Quick Settings tile and a Tasker/MacroDroid broadcast intent (rate-limited to 1/min). An optional **local HTTP server** serves realtime HC JSON on the LAN. Per-request logs are kept. License AGPL-3.0-only (store redistribution needs a commercial licence). About 177★, 228 commits — [repo](https://github.com/mcnaveen/health-connect-webhook); [docs/features.md](https://github.com/mcnaveen/health-connect-webhook/blob/main/docs/features.md)
- **Home Assistant Companion app (official)**: 36 Health Connect sensors. Activity: active and total calories, BMR, daily distance, elevation, floors, steps, hydration. Body: weight, height, body fat, lean mass, body water, bone mass. Heart: HR, resting HR, HRV, systolic and diastolic BP. Vitals: body and basal temperature, glucose, SpO2, respiratory rate, VO2max. Sleep: duration, start, end, and per-stage durations, with `stageTypes`/`stageStartTimes`/`stageEndTimes` attributes. Updates on the periodic 15-min sensor cycle and uses "only the last 30 days of data". Units include weight in grams and hydration in mL — [HA Companion docs: Sensors](https://companion.home-assistant.io/docs/core/sensors/)
- **Life Dashboard Companion** (F-Droid, plus a HACS integration paired by QR, or MQTT): 33 HC types, including HRV, VO2max, skin temperature and cycle tracking. 24 types become HA sensors, but event-style records do not. Dense HR can be downsampled to 1/5/15/60-min summaries. Daily totals come from HC aggregates, so phone and watch steps count once. Sync interval is ≥15 min or fixed times, with quiet hours — [F-Droid: Life Dashboard Companion](https://f-droid.org/en/packages/com.owen282000.lifedashboard/)
- **HealthConnect_to_HomeAssistant** (`AyraHikari`): pushes HR, sleep stages, steps, weight, body temperature, exercise, SpO2, hydration and calories to `sensor.health_connect` using an HA URL plus a long-lived token. Interval is configurable, with a 1–30-day sync range and WorkManager retries. GPL-3.0, about 45★ — [repo](https://github.com/AyraHikari/HealthConnect_to_HomeAssistant)
- **HealthConnectExports** (`angeloanan`): minimal Kotlin app that POSTs grouped JSON to an HTTP server. Last push June 2024, and the author says it won't be made "proper" (likely stale) — [repo](https://github.com/angeloanan/HealthConnectExports); [ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fangeloanan%2Fhealthconnectexports)
- **teqxnology/healthexport**: HC → Google Sheets or CSV (not self-hosted server) — [repo](https://github.com/teqxnology/healthexport)
- **HealthExportForAI**: user guide exists but details not reviewed — [guide](https://yhmr.github.io/HealthExportForAI/en/guide)

**Commercial**
- **Health Sync** (appyhapps, paid in-app, 7.9.x line in 2026, 7.9.2.1 uploaded 2026-08-17): background sync between Samsung Health, HC, Google Fit, Garmin, Fitbit and others. Since 7.9.0.1 it can export HC data to Google Drive as CSV, and activities as FIT/TCX/GPX/KML/CSV — [APKMirror 7.9.0.1](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/appyhapps-nl/health-sync/health-sync-7-9-0-1-release/); [AppGoblin listing](https://appgoblin.info/apps/nl.appyhapps.healthsync)

### Inferences
- **Recommended continuous pipeline:** Samsung Health (+ Galaxy Watch) → Health Connect → **Health Connect Webhook** app → HTTPS POST to an Odysseus ingestion endpoint on the NAS (API key, reachable via Tailscale or the LAN). Schedule it every few hours, and use a fixed nightly run to catch sleep. Grant the history permission once and backfill. Odysseus stores raw records idempotently (key on the HC record `metadata.id` plus data origin package), then builds daily rollups.
- **Use HA Companion sensors only for automations or "now" values,** not as the Health app's store. They expose only latest values or daily totals from a 30-day window, so HA recorder history would be a lossy, resampled copy.
- Treat **HC's Drive backup** as a disaster-recovery copy only. It goes to Google's cloud, which isn't privacy-first, and the ZIP format is undocumented.
- HC aggregate APIs dedupe overlapping sources (phone + watch), so an exporter that sends aggregates (daily steps) is simpler. One that sends raw records needs Odysseus to apply the same app-priority dedupe.

### Gaps
- No independent reliability data (missed syncs, Doze/battery-kill rates on One UI) for Health Connect Webhook or the HA Companion HC sensors. Reddit threads weren't surfaced by search.
- The Health Connect Webhook payload JSON schema wasn't fetched (it's in `docs/api-reference.md` / `docs/webhook.md`). Release cadence and last-commit date weren't visible.
- The format of the HC backup ZIP (likely an SQLite DB) is undocumented. No source found.

## 4. Self-hosted replacements and pipelines

### Takeaway
There is no true self-hosted **replacement** for Samsung Health on a Galaxy Watch. Gadgetbridge, the main FOSS companion app, does **not** support Samsung Galaxy Watches. It does support about 460 other devices (sum of its index counts) (Garmin, Huawei, Amazfit, Xiaomi, Pebble, Bangle.js, etc.), exports its SQLite DB automatically, and writes to Health Connect. The realistic privacy-first options are: (a) keep Samsung Health as the sensor app and siphon data out via HC plus manual exports into Odysseus; or (b) switch to a Gadgetbridge-supported watch with no vendor cloud. For heavier lifting, **Open Wearables** (MIT, Docker) is a full self-hosted normalisation API. **Fasten On-Prem** covers medical records, but its provider connectors are US-only.

### Cited Findings

**Gadgetbridge (FOSS companion, cloud-free)**
- The device index lists 35 vendor categories: Garmin 157, Huawei/Honor 66, Amazfit 58, Xiaomi 43, Moyoung 31, GloryFit 16, FitPro 13, Casio 12, Pebble 8, Fossil 5, Bangle.js 2, and Pine64, Withings, Coros, AsteroidOS and Wasp-os with 1 each. **Samsung Galaxy Watch is not listed** — [Gadgetbridge: wearables](https://gadgetbridge.org/gadgets/wearables/)
- An October 2026 roundup confirms Galaxy Watch, Fitbit, Pixel Watch and Apple Watch are absent, and that the only Samsung product listed is earbuds. Version 0.94.0 reached F-Droid on 2026-09-16 (Android 6+). It's not on Google Play. Some devices need an auth key extracted from the vendor app (secondary, Japanese summary of How-To Geek) — [smartwatchlife.jp](https://www.smartwatchlife.jp/82606/)
- **Auto export:** Settings → Automations → "Auto export database" / "Auto export zip", with a chosen path and frequency. The DB export is an SQLite file named `Gadgetbridge` (no extension) containing only the activity database. The ZIP export includes preferences and GPX. A "Run AutoExport now" button triggers it manually — [Gadgetbridge: Auto export](https://gadgetbridge.org/internals/automations/auto-export/); [Gadgetbridge: data management](https://gadgetbridge.org/internals/development/data-management)
- Community pipeline: auto-export to WebDAV/Nextcloud (overwrite mode), then a script reads the SQLite file and writes to InfluxDB — [Ben Tasker blog](https://bentasker.co.uk/posts/blog/software-development/linking-a-bip3-smartwatch-with-gadgetbridge-to-write-stats-to-influxdb.html); [gadgetbridge_to_influxdb](https://github.com/bentasker/gadgetbridge_to_influxdb)
- **Health Connect integration:** introduced in 0.89.0, distance added in 0.91.0, BP and failed-workout display in 0.95.x. It writes steps, distance, active calories, HR, RHR, HRV, SpO2, glucose, BP, body/skin temperature, respiratory rate, sleep, weight, VO2max, hydration and exercise sessions (with route, cadence, power, speed). It can sync after each device sync (off by default) — [Gadgetbridge: Health Connect](https://gadgetbridge.org/basics/integrations/health-connect/); [0.89.0](https://gadgetbridge.org/blog/release-0_89_00/); [0.95.1](https://gadgetbridge.org/blog/release-0_95_01/)

**Open Wearables (self-hosted unification API)**
- MIT licensed, about 2.8k★. Stack: FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis + Celery worker/beat, a SvelteKit portal, and optional S3/Svix/OTel. Deploy with `make init && docker compose up -d`. The docs warn to set your own admin password because the default is a known weak credential — [GitHub: the-momentum/open-wearables](https://github.com/the-momentum/open-wearables)
- Cloud providers: Garmin, Oura, Whoop, Polar, Suunto, Strava, Withings, Ultrahuman, SensorBio, Google Health, and Fitbit (deprecated). On-device sources via mobile SDKs: Apple Health, Health Connect, and **Samsung Health** (Kotlin Android SDK uploads to your instance). A built-in FastMCP server exposes activity, sleep, workouts, timeseries and cycles to MCP clients — [GitHub](https://github.com/the-momentum/open-wearables)
- Android support arrived in 0.3. The vendor says it is "live, but not zero-maintenance", and that Galaxy Watch data in Samsung Health may not be reflected in HC — [Momentum blog: Open Wearables 0.3](https://themomentum.ai/blog/open-wearables-0-3-android-google-health-connect-samsung-health-railway)

**Medical records**
- **Fasten On-Prem** (GPLv3, community maintained): self-hosted personal and family medical record. FHIR-based, SMART-on-FHIR OAuth to "650+" (US) healthcare providers, multi-user. Docker image `ghcr.io/fastenhealth/fasten-onprem:main` (port 9090→8080), plus a `sandbox` image with synthetic data. It generates its own CA on first start. Dashboards and wearable integration are listed as future work — [GitHub: fasten-onprem](https://github.com/fastenhealth/fasten-onprem/)

**Dashboards (InfluxDB/Grafana)**
- `garmin-grafana`: Dockerised Garmin Connect → InfluxDB → Grafana templates — [dev.co listing](https://dev.co/databases/open-source/garmin-grafana)
- iOS analogue: Health Export Pro → small receiver (API key) → InfluxDB (Flux) → Grafana, with a Docker Compose setup guide — [health-exporter setup guide](https://health-exporter.pages.dev/docs/setup-guides/influxdb-grafana/)
- Fitbit fetch → InfluxDB 1.11 (2.x support limited) → Grafana — [selfhostyourself.com](https://selfhostyourself.com/services/fitbit-health-dashboard)
- Security note: these projects have no audit logging or RBAC. Put webhooks behind an API key and HTTPS reverse proxy — [dev.co](https://dev.co/databases/open-source/garmin-grafana)

### Inferences
- **For Odysseus specifically, don't add InfluxDB/Grafana.** The Life hub already owns storage and UI. A health schema in the existing DB (raw records + daily rollups) plus Odysseus-native charts beats a parallel Grafana that can't integrate with Life's day view and search. Grafana-style projects are useful mainly as references for which panels people build: daily steps, RHR trend, sleep stages, weight.
- **Open Wearables is overkill for one user** (Postgres + Redis + Celery + portal). It's worth borrowing its normalised schema (daily summaries / sleep / workouts / timeseries / cycles) as a model for Odysseus tables. Its Samsung Health SDK path still needs an Android app, and building one isn't worth it when HC Webhook exists.
- **Replacement decision tree:**
  - Keep the Galaxy Watch → Samsung Health stays (needed for sensors, and in Brazil for Anvisa-cleared ECG/BP). Disable SH cloud sync if desired (but SH→HC needs "Sync with Samsung Account" per CareClinic; verify). Export via HC Webhook continuously plus SH manual export monthly.
  - Willing to change watch → a Gadgetbridge-supported device (well-supported Amazfit, a Garmin via Gadgetbridge, or Bangle.js/PineTime for fully open firmware) → Gadgetbridge auto-export SQLite to a Syncthing/WebDAV folder on the NAS → Odysseus importer. Also enable Gadgetbridge → HC so phone apps still see the data.
- Fasten has limited value in Brazil because its connectors are US providers. It could still be a viewer for FHIR bundles. Lab PDFs from Brazilian labs would be better handled as documents in Odysseus.

### Gaps
- Gadgetbridge's per-device support level for specific Amazfit or Garmin models in 2026 wasn't checked. Neither was whether any Wear OS (Galaxy Watch) FOSS companion exists. None was found.
- No source on a Home Assistant core integration for Samsung Health (none appears to exist). HA only receives HC data via the Companion app or community apps.
- Didn't evaluate Brazilian medical-record sources (Meu SUS Digital / RNDS FHIR) for import. Out of scope, but potentially relevant.
- Couldn't verify whether SH → HC sync works with Samsung Account cloud sync turned off. CareClinic says the account-sync toggle is required.

## 5. Google Fit shutdown and migration, and the impact on Takeout data

### Takeaway
Google is retiring Google Fit in 2026. The Fit APIs (including REST) are "deprecated in 2026" with no direct REST replacement, and the consumer app is being folded into **Google Health**, the rebranded Fitbit app, with a promised "migrate your data" invite "later this year". No exact shutdown date had been published as of the latest sources found. The user's existing Takeout archive is the durable copy. Future Android data should come via Health Connect (on-device), not Google's cloud.

### Cited Findings
- Official FAQ: "The Google Fit APIs, including the Google Fit REST API, will be deprecated in 2026". New sign-ups have been closed since May 1, 2024. After the migration period "Google Fit APIs will no longer be available". "There is no alternative to the Fit REST API". The suggested replacements are **Health Connect** (on-device) and the **Google Health API** (cloud, account-centric) — [Android Developers: Fit migration FAQ](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/migration/fit/faq)
- Vendor reading: support "until the end of 2026", with no specific date published — [Sahha blog, 2026-08-26 (vendor)](https://sahha.ai/blog/google-fit-api-sunset-migration/)
- On May 7, 2026, 9to5Google reported Google Fit "will shut down" in favour of Google Health, with the quote "We'll invite Google Fit users to migrate their data into Google Health app later this year". No exact date, and no list of which data types transfer — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/05/07/google-fit-shut-down-health-replacement-migration-tool-coming/)
- The Fitbit app began rolling out as "Google Health" on May 19, 2026, as an automatic upgrade. Fitbit accounts not migrated to Google accounts by July 15, 2026 face deletion — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/googles-fitbit-app-is-becoming-google-health-and-fit-is-finally-finished_id180179); [BGR](https://bgr.com/2168254/google-changing-fitbit-app-rebrand/)
- The legacy Fitbit Web API shutdown date moved several times: 30 Sep, then 30 Oct, then 2 Nov 2026 (vendor blogs, conflicting) — [Sahha Fitbit sunset (2026-10-29)](https://sahha.ai/blog/fitbit-api-sunset-migration/); [Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/blog/fitbit-shutdown-september-2026-migration-options); [Open Wearables guide](https://openwearables.io/blog/fitbit-web-api-shutdown-2026-migration-guide)
- At announcement, the migration tool didn't exist yet and Google hadn't said which data types transfer — [Gadget Hacks](https://android.gadgethacks.com/news/google-fit-to-google-health-migration-plannedbut-key-details-missing/)
- Third-party guides recommend exporting Fit data via **Google Takeout** before shutdown. No automatic history migration is guaranteed (vendor) — [fitmesh.fit](https://www.fitmesh.fit/en/blog/google-health-replaces-google-fit); [Mindbowser](https://www.mindbowser.com/fetch-google-fit-data-continuously)
- Health Connect is Android-only and does not import Google Fit cloud history. The Fit History API maps to the Google Health API (phone) or HC (Wear OS) for developers (vendor) — [Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/google-fit-api-sunset-migration/)

### Inferences
- **The user's Takeout archive (2018–2026) is now irreplaceable.** Archive the raw ZIP on the NAS immutably, alongside the Odysseus import. Re-run Takeout once more before Fit is switched off to capture the final weeks. After shutdown, Takeout's "Fit" product will likely disappear or be replaced by a Google Health export.
- After the Fit shutdown, Google-cloud data will live in Google Health. That isn't privacy-first, and its API is cloud OAuth. For Odysseus the canonical continuous source should be **Health Connect on the phone**, with Google Health as optional legacy.
- Mapping continuity: Takeout "Move Minutes"/"Heart Points" have no Samsung Health equivalent. Health Connect's `ActivityIntensityRecord` (moderate/vigorous minutes, `INTENSITY_MINUTES_TOTAL`) is the closest modern field ([Android Developers data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)). Odysseus should define its own "minutos ativos" metric computed consistently across sources, and label the 2018–2026 Fit series as "Google Fit Move Minutes" to avoid mixing definitions.

### Gaps
- No source confirms whether the Google Fit → Google Health migration tool shipped by November 2026, or gives the exact date the Fit app and APIs shut off.
- No source on whether Google Takeout's "Fit" export will remain available after shutdown, or what a Google Health Takeout contains.
- Availability of the Google Health app and its features in Brazil wasn't checked.
